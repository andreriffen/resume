# Design, AI, and the Work of Making Things Real

AI can produce interface ideas quickly. A generated screen, however, is only a proposal; it does not know whether the flow fits the product, the users, or the system behind it.

## An idea still needs context

For example, a tool can suggest a dashboard layout. Someone still has to decide what information matters, what happens when data is missing, how errors are explained, and how the layout works on a small screen. Those choices depend on product rules and real constraints, not just appearance.

AI can be useful for exploring alternatives or drafting placeholder content. Treat the output as a starting point: check it against the actual requirements, test the interaction, and replace assumptions with product-specific decisions.

## Design engineering connects the pieces

The work does not stop at a polished mockup. Turning an idea into an interface means connecting visual decisions to components, application state, APIs, responsive behavior, and accessibility. That is where design and engineering meet: not in making a handoff “seamless,” but in making the intent survive implementation.

Accessibility is one example where a confident-looking result is not enough. The [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) define testable criteria for accessible web content. A generated interface still needs to be checked against those criteria and against the needs of its users.

## A useful role for AI

The value is not that AI replaces design judgment or guarantees better experiences. It can help widen the set of ideas to consider. People still need to choose, validate, and implement the right one.

That balance keeps the tool in its place: useful for exploration, but not a substitute for understanding the problem or taking responsibility for the result.
